# Ethics, safety, and misuse risks

## Intended use

Competency Gap Intelligence is intended for transparent development planning and research.

It is not a performance-management engine, promotion score, hiring screen, disciplinary tool, or automated employment decision system.

## Missing evidence is not a deficit

A person may lack an observed competency record because they have not had an opportunity to demonstrate the skill.

The system therefore does not convert missing evidence into proficiency zero.

Unknown evidence should trigger an assessment or evidence-collection step, not an assumption of deficiency.

## Evidence quality

Self-ratings, manager ratings, tests, work samples, portfolios, and observed performance do not have identical strengths or biases.

The system keeps evidence source, date, assessor, and confidence visible so that reviewers can challenge weak inputs.

A confidence value is itself a human or methodological judgment and should not be treated as objective truth.

## Opportunity-to-demonstrate bias

Competency evidence depends on access to assignments, tools, mentoring, projects, and decision authority.

Two people with similar capability may have different evidence because one had more opportunity to demonstrate it.

Do not use an evidence gap as proof of lower potential.

## Role-profile bias

Role requirements can reflect:

- outdated job design
- inflated expectations
- local preferences
- unnecessary credentialism
- manager-specific norms

Every role profile should have provenance, versioning, and periodic review.

## Development is not punishment

A competency gap should not automatically become mandatory training.

Alternative responses can include:

- practice opportunities
- mentoring
- job aids
- workflow redesign
- role clarification
- supervised assignments
- changes to tools or process

The system currently matches learning resources only; a real development process should consider these wider options.

## Employment decisions

Do not use this prototype alone for:

- hiring or rejection
- promotion
- termination
- pay
- disciplinary action
- performance ratings
- succession decisions
- forced ranking

Those uses require substantially stronger validation, governance, legal review, and human decision processes.

## Sensitive attributes

Do not infer protected or sensitive characteristics from competency evidence.

Do not use sensitive attributes as hidden proxies for development priority.

When fairness evaluation is appropriate and lawful, examine whether evidence availability, scoring, opportunities, and recommendations differ systematically across groups.

## Privacy

Competency systems can accumulate detailed histories of work, assessments, feedback, and manager judgments.

Collect only what is needed.

Define retention, access, correction, deletion, and appeal processes.

Avoid storing raw confidential work products when a validated score or evidence reference is sufficient.

## Explainability

A person affected by a development recommendation should be able to see:

- the role target
- the scale
- the evidence used
- evidence age
- confidence/review flags
- the calculated gap
- importance
- prerequisite blockers
- why a resource matched

If this chain cannot be explained, the recommendation should not be treated as authoritative.

## Before real use

Document the competency framework, role-profile governance, evidence rubric, assessor process, scale anchors, reliability, missing-evidence treatment, recency policy, ranking sensitivity, resource validation, privacy controls, review/appeal path, accessibility, and clear boundaries preventing development analytics from silently becoming employment scoring.
